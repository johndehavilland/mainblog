I asked AI the other day how many calories were in a single sheet of Pepper Ridge Farm puff pastry. It confidently told me 300. I didn't double-check and ate the whole tomato pie I made (which was delicious). The reality is a single sheet is 900 calories and for a moment I realized I had trusted a word prediction engine and it had failed me.

AI generated content is everywhere. 1 in 4 social media posts are flagged as AI (with posts on LinkedIn being the biggest offender) (according)[https://www.pangram.com/blog/ai-in-your-feed] to Pangram (a maker of a chrome extension that flags posts as AI or not for you). Its showing up in the powerpoints at work, in your emails, in blog posts and even in local restaurant promotions.

[example of fake vs. actual with bonaparte castle]

Here is an interesting thought exercise. If I use AI to craft you an email about a topic, and you use AI to write a response - what value am I, or you, bringing here. In the era of AI, its vitally important we don't lose what makes us Human - our ability to think critically. When I use AI, I sometimes pause for a minute to think - if I could automate AI doing these tasks, would this still work? If the answer is yes, then great - automate it but don't pretend you are needed in that process any more.

We are starting to get to a point where we can tell AI generated content. We see the tells. The overly, long-winded sentences not saying much at all. The same vapid points repeated multiple different ways, the sameness of the Powerpoint template with too many words, too many buzz words and not enough substance.

Look, AI today is fundamentally a system that, given a long enough time horizon and enough of a sample size a sameness will emerge. An English class' students saw this first hand, when asked to use AI to generate an unique essay topic, upon reading them out loud in the class they realized it all sounded the same (https://lithub.com/what-happened-when-i-tried-to-replace-myself-with-chatgpt-in-my-english-classroom/)

A while ago I wrote about how we shouldn't let the word prediction engines fool us. The spirit of human endeavour and creativeness cannot be bound solely by a convergence of what is currently known but needs to break free. And to do that we cannot sacrifice critical thinking. We cannot sacrifice sitting with a blank page, scratching the first marks. Because if we do, then we are missing the point.

This is not a post against AI - far from it. I am a huge AI advocate and I fundamentally believe it is and will change the fabric of society in material ways. But we have to stop thinking of AI as way to outsource our creativity or our critical thinking. We need to use AI to sharpen that for us, to make us better, to challenge us, to push us in ways we hadn't thought possible. But we have to start with a human grounded perspective first.

Ask AI to challenge you. You could land a draft of your thesis and ask AI to offer arguments against it. Instead of "write a presentation for x", instead ask "what questions should I answer before writing my presentation". You could ask AI to argue from different lens, e.g. an economist or a engineer. One powerful use of AI is to have it employ the socratic method (https://aimaker.substack.com/p/i-built-socratic-ai-that-questions-every-decision-i-make-here-what-i-learned). Have AI ask you questions, challenge your assumptions. This is powerful because you don't surrender your voice or your critical thinking, but instead expand it.


Our ability to do complex work builds on doing simple work first, if we stop doing that we start losing that muscle.

Think of AI as handling:

Information gathering
Organization
Drafting
Explaining concepts
Brainstorming

While you handle:

Defining the problem
Judging evidence
Setting priorities
Making value-based decisions
Taking responsibility for the outcome
